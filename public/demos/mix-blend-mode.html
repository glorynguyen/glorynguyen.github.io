⚠️ Low contrast! Text color is too close to the background — difference struggles near mid-tones.
⚡ Limitations of mix-blend-mode: difference
  • Mid-gray dead zone — Background near #808080 (128,128,128) produces text color #7f7f7f — only 1 unit of contrast per channel. Text becomes virtually invisible.
  • White text on white bg = invisible — |255 − 255| = 0 on all channels. The text becomes fully black… wait, no — it becomes #000000, but the bg is #ffffff, so it's actually visible. The truly invisible case is when text color matches the background after blending.
  • No guaranteed WCAG contrast — Unlike normal text, there is no way to ensure a minimum 4.5:1 contrast ratio. The resulting color is entirely determined by the background, so accessibility cannot be guaranteed across all colors.
  • Not supported on all elements equally — Works on text and inline elements, but has inconsistent behavior with position: fixed, isolation: isolate, and hardware-accelerated layers (will-change, transform: translateZ(0)).
  • Performance cost — The browser must composite blend calculations per-pixel every frame. On large areas or during animations, this can cause jank on lower-end devices.
  • Gradients & images are unpredictable — On non-uniform backgrounds, different parts of the same text render in different colors. This can look cool but can also be unreadable in certain regions.
  • Print stylesheets — Most print renderers ignore blend modes entirely, so text may appear as plain white on a colored background (invisible on paper).
